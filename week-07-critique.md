# 9. What I Learned

The most useful part of the review was seeing the portfolio from someone else's perspective.

Because I had spent a lot of time working on the site, some problems were no longer obvious to me.

The reviewer helped me identify whether my positioning was immediately understandable and whether the portfolio actually supported my claim.

I also learned that feedback should not automatically become a list of things to implement.

I separated feedback based on impact:

**Must-fix = affects clarity, credibility, usability, or the main action.**

**Nice-to-have = useful improvement that can wait.**

Instead of defending my original decisions, I focused on whether the portfolio was doing its job for the visitor.
